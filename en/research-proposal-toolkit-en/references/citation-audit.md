# Citation auditing and critical synthesis

## 1. Purpose and timing

Create `citation-ledger.md` in the student's project directory. Record why each source appears, how closely it was read, what it actually supports, and which judgments belong to the review author. This is an auditable evidence record, not a collection of abstracts or a ranking of mandatory citations.

Start when Task 01 uses sources. Task 03 must cover all actual review citations; Task 04 adds sources used in the proposal. Record even background-only or apparently decorative citations honestly. Separate candidates, currently cited sources, and removed sources. Keep a brief removal reason without retaining deleted items in the active bibliography.

## 2. Compact index and source cards

Use one index row per source: `stable ID | short title | citation status | reading depth | review/proposal locations | purpose | retain/verify/remove recommendation`.

Put details in short cards rather than an excessively wide table. Link preprint and published versions of the same work, deduplicate, and retain the version actually read. Bibliography renumbering must not change the stable ID.

Use the seven short fields below for ordinary sources. Typically, about 100–150 English words of commentary, excluding bibliographic details and links, are enough; the Chinese counterpart uses about 200–300 characters. These are guidelines for brevity, not rigid limits. Expand important analysis in a focused record rather than deleting needed evidence. Mark missing information as unverified, not applicable, or inaccessible—never invent values.

### R001 | Full article title (template, not a real source)

- **Identity:** Authors, year, full journal/conference name, publication/preprint status, DOI or original-source link, and version read.
- **Impact context:** Journal impact factor with metric year/definition, source, and retrieval date; conference rank with scheme/version, source, and date; article citation count with database and retrieval date. Mark unavailable or inapplicable fields separately.
- **Reading record:** Depth, reader (student/assistant), date, and sections checked; close reading: yes/partial/no, with a basis rather than a checkbox alone.
- **One-sentence summary:** Paraphrase the object, approach or position, and main finding. Label summaries based only on the abstract.
- **Why cited here:** Draft, section/paragraph ID, exact claim supported, and citation role. Record different uses separately.
- **Evidence and limits:** Original page/section/figure/algorithm, what it supports, and what it does not.
- **Assessment and action:** Relationship to other work or this project; retain, reread, replace, or remove with reasons. Link substantive analysis to a focused record.

Reading states may be bibliographic information only, abstract skim, selected sections checked, key methods/results checked, or full close reading. Record code inspection and actual reproduction separately. Do not infer close reading from an old “read” label; mark access restrictions.

## 3. Limits of impact information

Venue names establish identity. An impact factor describes a journal, not the quality of the individual article. Conference ranks need a named scheme and version; do not force a ranking on fields without a relevant scheme. Citation counts depend on database, version merging, and date. Distinguish zero, not found, and inaccessible.

Prefer traceable metric publishers or databases. State the source actually used; do not present unofficial statistics as official. Leave unverifiable values open. Keep a dated snapshot instead of refreshing on every writing pass; update when new checks, user requests, or submission review require it.

Metrics provide context, not evidence for claims or a rule for inclusion. Do not require a source because of prestige, exclude recent work for low citation counts, or combine incomparable metrics into a universal quality score.

## 4. Make citation purposes auditable

Roles include concept/history, background, method, baseline, direct support, counterexample/debate, scope limitation, and data/material provenance. “Relevant to the topic” is insufficient: specify which claim in the paragraph needs the source.

If the current reason is simply breadth or citation count without independent support, mark it as a **potentially redundant citation** and recommend checking, merging, or removing it. Record existing uses instead of inventing necessity. Resolve redundancy before submission; this rule does not endorse padding. Legitimate background citations are not automatically redundant.

Create one identity card for a source cited in multiple places and record each purpose. A narrow finding cannot support an entire paragraph of broader claims. Preserve conflicting results and their conditions, not only sources favorable to the proposed project.

## 5. Focused reading and analysis

For the closest methods, key theory, major counterexamples, or sources actually analyzed in depth, add a `Focused record | R001` section in the same ledger and link it from the short card. Do not require a long note for every paper.

Separate:

1. **Source facts:** Question, assumptions, method/argument, findings, and original locations.
2. **Comparison:** Substantive differences from other work and whether objects, metrics, and conditions are comparable.
3. **Review author's judgment:** Evidence-to-judgment reasoning, limits, rival explanations, and implications for this project.
4. **Open issues:** Access/reading gaps, details to verify, and gaps that cannot yet be claimed.
5. **Writing use:** Target sections, analysis already used in the draft, and ideas remaining internal discussion.

Date and attribute analysis to the student, assistant, or supervisor feedback. Supervisor opinions are not findings of the cited paper. Preserve useful negative evidence; when a judgment changes, record why rather than silently converting it into a new fact.

## 6. Use before and after drafting

Before drafting a section, read relevant cards and focused records, assess evidential sufficiency, and recheck primary sources for important details. Organize prose around the current topic; do not paste ledger cards together as a review. Afterward, update locations, purposes, attribution, and new close-reading findings.

Before submission, reconcile both ways: every in-text citation has a bibliography entry and card; bibliography entries are used; cards marked cited occur in the current draft. Flag strong claims supported only by shallow reading, metrics without dates/sources, padding, duplicates, and claim-source mismatches for human review.

The ledger is excluded from the roughly ten-page review body. It exposes gaps but does not automatically certify truth or novelty.


Evidence relationship: record whether each use provides direct support, indirect support, counterevidence, or background. Do not upgrade indirect evidence into direct validation.
